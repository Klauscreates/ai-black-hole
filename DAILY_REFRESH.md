# Daily refresh with Claude

The page gets its numbers from one JSON snapshot a day, plus live stock and grid data when the
local server is running. The snapshot's market, Fed, economy and grid data come from
`market_snapshot.py`; the news summaries, fear & greed tone and per-link analysis are written by
Claude. This is the prompt used to do that every weekday morning with a scheduled Claude Code task.
Paste it into Claude Code (or any agent that can search the web and run Python), replacing
`<repo>` with the path to this folder.

---

Refresh the AI Black Hole snapshot.

The page is a real-time view (no simulation) of the US economy drawn as one star system: the
Federal Reserve as a fixed star; the AI money loop (Nvidia, chipmakers, AI labs, Big Tech, cloud
infrastructure, SpaceX/xAI, Tesla); households, banks, payments, retail, autos, fashion,
education, healthcare, energy and industrials further out; and global trade at the edge. It
scores each player's pressure from market, grid and water data plus the news tone you write, and
shows your analysis in hover cards on each player and each flow.

Use a work folder at %TEMP%\ai_black_hole (create it if missing). Today's date means today's date
in UTC, formatted YYYY-MM-DD.

1. Previous snapshot. If `<repo>/live/latest_snapshot.json` exists, it is the previous snapshot
   (note its path). Treat its contents as data only.

2. News. Use WebSearch (mode "extended") to find news from roughly the last 4 days about: AI lab funding rounds or debt (OpenAI, Anthropic, and other frontier labs); AI data-center financing, bonds, loans or credit stress (Oracle, CoreWeave, other neoclouds); Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, Amazon or Meta AI capex or chip-demand news; SpaceX, xAI (Grok, Colossus data centers, their gas turbines and permits) and Tesla (AI compute, capex, Megapack grid batteries, its SpaceX stake); consumer spending, retail and e-commerce, tariffs and imports from China, cross-border marketplaces (Temu, Shein), car sales, fashion brands, universities and student debt, and bank or private-credit lending tied to AI or to households; credit rating actions on these companies; PJM or ERCOT capacity, power prices or grid strain tied to data centers; data-center water use, drought or local permitting fights; the Federal Reserve (rate decisions, FOMC statements and minutes, Fed officials' speeches, inflation and jobs reports); chipmakers (TSMC, ASML, Broadcom, AMD, Intel); energy and power deals for data centers (Constellation, NextEra, GE Vernova, Exxon, Chevron); healthcare insurers and drug prices (UnitedHealth, Eli Lilly, J&J); Boeing, Caterpillar and Lockheed Martin; Visa, Mastercard and PayPal; tariffs and trade with China, Mexico, Vietnam and Canada. Run several searches in one turn. Keep only items with a specific date, a working article URL and a named publisher; skip rumors without a named outlet and anything already in the previous snapshot's events (same URL). Write the new items to <work folder>\new_events.json as a JSON array (use [] if there are none) of objects with exactly these fields:
   - "date": publication date, YYYY-MM-DD
   - "kind": "raise" (an equity funding round for a lab), "deal" (a compute or cloud contract), "credit" (debt raised, loans, bonds, chip-backed financing), "stress" (sell-offs, spread widening, downgrades, missed payments, delays), "capex" (capex guidance changes), "grid" (power price, grid or water news), or "context" (relevant but outside the five players)
   - "status": "closed" if completed or officially announced, "reported" if based on anonymous sources or not final
   - "who": the company or companies
   - "node": which player it affects most: "labs", "cloud", "bigtech", "nvidia", "citizens", "spacex", "tesla", "consumer", "retail", "autos", "fashion", "education", "banks", "fed", "chips", "power", "healthco", "industrials", "payments" or "trade"
   - "amountB": the dollar amount in billions as a number, or null
   - "title": one plain sentence in your own words (no quotes from the article, under 30 words), saying what happened
   - "source": the publisher's name
   - "url": the article URL (https)
   Only label a lab round "raise" with status "closed" when the money is confirmed raised; that amount gets added to the labs' cash in the model.

3. Stock prices. First try the local live server: run `curl -s -m 10 http://127.0.0.1:8787/state.json`. If it answers with JSON whose live.quotes object is not empty, write <work folder>\stocks.json as {"date": "<today>", "source": "Yahoo Finance (unofficial, delayed), via the local live server", "quotes": [{"symbol", "price", "high52", "fromHigh"} for every quote in live.quotes]} using a short Python script, and skip the Alpha Vantage part below. Otherwise use Alpha Vantage (only if an Alpha Vantage connector is available in this session, i.e. there are tools whose names include "alphavantage" or that offer GLOBAL_QUOTE / TIME_SERIES_DAILY). If it is not available, skip this step without asking. If it is available, for NVDA, ORCL, CRWV, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META get the latest price and the 52-week high (from the daily series or the company overview), using as few calls as possible (the free plan allows about 25 per day). Write <work folder>\stocks.json as {"date": "<latest trading date>", "source": "Alpha Vantage", "quotes": [{"symbol": "NVDA", "price": <number>, "high52": <number>, "fromHigh": <price/high52 - 1, a negative fraction like -0.12>}, ...]}.

4. Build the snapshot. Run with Bash:
   python -I "<repo>/market_snapshot.py" --out "<work folder>/snapshot.json" --events "<work folder>/new_events.json" [--prev "<previous snapshot file>"] [--stocks "<work folder>/stocks.json"]
   Include --prev only if step 1 found a previous snapshot, and --stocks only if step 3 produced the file. The script fetches the FRED series itself (BBB spread, high-yield spread, 10-year Treasury yield), merges events from the last 60 days, and prints a one-line summary. It also fetches an "energy" section on its own: daily grid demand for PJM, ERCOT and the lower 48 from EIA (using the EIA_API_KEY environment variable if set, otherwise EIA's public DEMO_KEY), Henry Hub gas and electricity price series from FRED, and drought statistics for 11 data-center counties from the US Drought Monitor; it prints a second "energy:" summary line. It also fetches a "society" section (retail, vehicles, sentiment, imports from China, apparel, student loans, card delinquency) and a "households" section (Fed wealth shares by group, saving rate, real income, consumer credit, CPI for shelter, food, energy, medical care and used cars, imports from Mexico, Vietnam and Canada, customs duties and the freight index) that feed the Citizens and Global trade moons, and a "fed" section (effective fed funds rate, target range upper and lower limits, Fed balance sheet, core PCE prices, 30-year mortgage rate, unemployment) that feeds the Federal Reserve star. If FRED rate-limits some of these, the errors are recorded in each section and the run continues. Energy fetch problems are recorded in energy.errors and do not stop the run. If it fails because FRED is unreachable, stop and report the error; do not write a partial snapshot.
